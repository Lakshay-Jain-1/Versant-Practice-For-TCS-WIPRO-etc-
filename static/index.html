How to score well

  • Keep going if you miss a word. The whole sentence is scored. Smooth speech with one small word dropped beats a perfect but hesitant repeat.
  • Copy the rhythm. Match the speaker's pace and intonation. Not faster, not slower.
  • Chunk it. Hear long sentences as 3-4 word groups: "All students / must submit / their assignments / before the final deadline."
  • Small words count. Try to keep "a", "the", "to", "for" in place.
  • Start speaking straight away once the audio stops, before the memory fades.

Voice typing setup

  • Press Win+H once by hand to allow the microphone and set the language to English.
  • Keep this browser window in front when the audio ends, otherwise the helper will not press Win+H.
  • If voice typing does not open, click the answer box and use Start voice typing.

How to score well

  • Find who, what, where, when and why while reading. Also note the result at the end.
  • Understand, don't memorize. Word-for-word memorizing usually fails. Follow the story instead.
  • Keep all the details. A one-line summary scores low. Names, days, numbers and reasons all count.
  • Keep it simple. Short, correct sentences beat complex ones with mistakes.
  • Check spelling and full stops in the last 10 seconds.

    Useful phrases

    • Open: I am writing to... / I am writing regarding...
    • Apologize: I sincerely apologize for... / Please accept my apologies for...
    • Request: Could you please... / I would be grateful if you could...
    • Propose: Would it be possible to meet on...? / I am available on...
    • Close: Please let me know if you need any further information. / Thank you for your understanding.
    • Sign off: Best regards, / Sincerely, then your name.
